# Evidence quality: the overlooked layer in private-credit decisions

*Cygnus Private Credit Research · November 2026 · Educational primer*

Private credit depends on more than a plausible collateral value or a mathematically correct loan calculation. A defensible financing assessment must distinguish **what the evidence proves**, **what it suggests**, and **what remains unresolved**.

## Five questions before using an asset as collateral

**What is the asset?** Identity and ownership information should be checked against appropriate source records. An address, identifier or descriptive label alone may not establish the legal collateral position.

**How was its value established?** Valuation date, methodology, market assumptions and uncertainty matter. Two seemingly precise figures can measure different concepts or periods.

**Which obligations have priority?** Liens, senior claims and enforceability can materially affect the lender's recoverable position. A valuable asset is not necessarily freely available collateral.

**Can the borrower service the obligation?** Asset value and repayment capacity answer different questions. Cash-flow and debt-service evidence must be evaluated separately from an appraisal.

**What happens under stress?** Time-to-liquidity, transaction friction, price dispersion, borrower concentration and market conditions can change materially between normal and stressed regimes.

## Where AI may help—and where it must stop

AI may be useful for organizing evidence, locating inconsistencies, extracting structured claims and identifying matters needing review. But a well-formatted answer is not proof that a financial assertion is supported. In particular, contradictory documents, unknown number conventions and missing material inputs can make confident-looking results unsafe to rely upon.

A responsible workflow should preserve source references, represent uncertainty, reject unsupported inferences and route unresolved cases for appropriate human review. Automated models should not be presumed to approve, issue or fund credit merely because they can describe a calculation.

## A better question for financial AI

Instead of asking only *“Did the model produce a number?”*, researchers should also ask:

- Is the number justified by the available evidence?
- Are units, dates, identifiers and number conventions unambiguous?
- Can the result be reproduced under the stated assumptions?
- Does the system distinguish “unknown,” “conflicting,” “malformed,” and “unsupported” evidence?
- Does the workflow stop and escalate when evidence is insufficient?

## Scope and limitations

This is a conceptual educational discussion, **not an empirical study**, a proprietary THAL3S 16 benchmark result, or a substitute for legal, valuation or credit expertise. It does not quantify model accuracy, underwriting performance or financing speed. Specific applications require jurisdiction- and transaction-specific evidence and appropriate professional review.
